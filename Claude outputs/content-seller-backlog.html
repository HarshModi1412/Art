Fifty-three things
worth arguing about

A pass over the whole platform — the seller app in D:\Claude\Art and the storefronts it publishes at /s/<handle> — read three ways: as an Office Hours grilling of the value proposition, as a UI/UX audit of both surfaces, and against what the research says actually breaks small sellers on Shopify.

Nothing here is a decision. Score each item, argue with the scores, then mark Build / Later / Skip and the page keeps your marks.

The uncomfortable one

The product is twelve doors and no answer. Analytics, supply, catalogue, site, orders, content, ads — every module is equally prominent and none of them tells the seller what to do this morning. The Approval panel is the right idea, buried two clicks deep.

The wedge is backwards

You lead with the website builder — the one thing Shopify, Dukaan and Wix have already won. Your actual edge is RFM, win-back, complaint mining, positioning and EOQ on sales the seller already has. Lead with that; give the site away.

What you can actually take

Merchants describe app costs as "worse than the mafia" — free → $29/mo → a percentage of sales, and "you don't know what you don't know" about which you need. You replace four to six of them for one flat number. That is the pricing page.

How each item is scored

V — Value
1–5. How much money, time or churn this moves for the seller or their shopper.
S — Strength
1–5. How much it changes what the product feels like. 1 = cosmetic, 5 = repositioning.
R — Reach
1–3. How many users hit it. 3 = everyone, every session. 1 = a minority.
E — Effort
S/M/L → 1/2/3. Your build days, not story points.

Priority = round( V × S × R ÷ E ÷ 75 × 100 )  ·  band: ≥50 high · 25–49 medium · <25 low

Effort is the denominator on purpose. A cheap change that shifts perception beats an expensive one that shifts a number.

If you only ship eight things

Picked for the ratio of perceived change to build days, not for score alone. Together they take the product from "twelve modules" to "one thing that works", and they are roughly six weeks.

  1. Pick one seller and write one sentenceClothing sellers doing ₹2–20L a month across Instagram, Amazon and one own site. Every screen, every price, every post speaks to her. "Content Seller" is not that sentence.
  2. Price against the app stack, out loud"Replaces Lifetimely + Stocky + Judge.me + a win-back app + your inventory spreadsheet. ₹999. One number, no percentage of sales." That's the whole landing page.
  3. "See it with sample data" on every empty moduleThe demo endpoint already exists. Right now it only serves the landing page. Nobody should have to find a CSV to see what you built.
  4. A "Today" strip above the tilesThree lines: 2 new orders, 4 items below reorder point, 12 customers slipping away. Each one clicks straight into the action. This is the product; the tiles are the filing cabinet.
  5. Guest checkout on storefrontsForcing a first-time COD buyer on a phone to invent a password is the most expensive line in the codebase. Create the account silently from the phone number after the order.
  6. UPI at checkoutCOD-only both loses orders and pays for every RTO. Razorpay is already wired on the seller side — reuse the keys.
  7. WhatsApp-shaped sharingPer-product title, description and OG image so a pasted store link renders a card. In Indian D2C the share sheet is the storefront.
  8. Seed the site on first entryBrand, products, photos and copy already exist in Product Management. Step three should open on a finished site the seller edits — not five steps of blank fields.

Already shipped this session

SITE-16 — storefront sign-in typography. The shopper login on published sites was borrowing the seller's display face for its tabs and leaving the input fields to the body face. On a store running Anton, Bebas, Italiana or Bodoni that turned "Log in / Sign up" into decoration at 12px, and on Cormorant it rendered the password field as thin grey hairlines.

The greeting still uses the brand's display face — capped at 700 weight, tracking clamped, forced out of all-caps so a wide face reads as a welcome rather than a banner. Everything that does work — tabs, labels, inputs, errors — moved to --fa, the small UI face the seller already picked, with extra letter-spacing on the password field so the dots can be counted. No colour, size or face is hard-coded: it is the same three type roles, reassigned to the right jobs.

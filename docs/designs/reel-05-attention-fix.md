## Problem Statement

The user scores reels with a TRIBE v2 Colab notebook (predicted brain response per second: Visual, Audio, Language, Attention, Overall). Reel 5 v3 (`instagram/hyperframes/videos/reel-05-drain`, 29.0 s) still shows two dips: D1 0:00-0:07 (the hook) and D2 0:23-0:29 (end card). The best moment is 0:16, the 1T plug slam. The user wants a crazy hook, a clear link from the hook to the message, and no dips.

## What the notebook actually measures (verified in its code)

- Each brain system is smoothed over 3 s, then turned into a z-score against **the same video's own** mean and spread. Overall is the z-scored mean of the four.
- A second counts as low when `Overall < max(-0.5, 20th percentile of the same video)`, with 1 s gaps bridged and **no minimum length**.
- So about 20% of seconds are always flagged: at least 6 seconds of any 30 s reel, however good it is. "Zero dips" is not reachable by editing.
- The first 3-4 seconds start handicapped: predicted brain response takes seconds to build (the notebook's own Sintel example: visual cortex lights up at t=4 s). Both Reel 5 runs had their lowest values at the very start.
- Because scores are relative, the hook rises above the -0.5 line only if something else in the video sits lower. Rough model with v3's numbers: if the last ~6 s drop far enough, the hook moves from about -0.9 to about -0.3 and stops being flagged.

## What v3's chart says, system by system

- Visual: climbs to +1.55 at 0:15 (month grid, plug), then crashes to -1.5 in the night scene (0:17-0:23: dark, mostly static text) and on the paper end card.
- Audio: flat around -0.3 for 0:02-0:07, peaks at the plug (riser, impact), falls to -1.9 at the end.
- Language: -1.5 for the first 6 s (warm-up), peaks at +1.4 during the offer (0:20).
- Attention: -1.3 at the start, +1.3 from 0:11 to 0:16.

## Reference reels (the user's downloads in D:\Claude)

All four open with 1-2 s of real camera footage with strong motion (BMW drift, a cat jumping in a car, a rolling ball, paper ripping), then cut to fast full-frame text cards, about one per second, with alternating background colors.

## Premises (agreed)

1. Zero dips can't be reached in this notebook. Target: hook and offer above the line; the weakest ~20% lands in the outro, after the call to action.
2. Judge the hook by comparing versions (v2 hook about -1.4, v3 about -1.0) and against Instagram's 3-second hold rate, not against zero.
3. Real camera footage beats illustration for this model. The best hook is a real phone clip that IS the message (coins swirling down a real sink drain), plus fast color text cards.
4. Borrowed clips risk a muted or blocked reel (Instagram rights matching) and read as an endorsement when a stranger's face is in an ad. Copy their hook formats, not their footage.

## Approaches Considered

- **A. Re-sequence (minimal, S):** v3 visuals; harder open; CTA earlier; a calm final ~4 s that takes the notebook's 20%. Ships today, but the hook stays illustrated.
- **B. Real-drain hook plus reference-reel rhythm (ideal, M):** A, plus a 2-3 s real phone clip of coins down a real sink that match-cuts into the illustrated drain, plus full-frame color word cards. Waits on one clip from the user.
- **C. Benchmark mode for the notebook (lateral, S):** score the reel against a viral reel's distribution so "dip" means weaker than a benchmark. Changes the measurement, not the reel.

## Recommended Approach: B in two stages

**Stage 1 (now, no new footage):**

On "sixteen" a full-frame black card with the gold $1,641 counter, then back to the vortex as the number is sucked down the drain. The cuts, the color flashes and the full-field brightness changes copy the reference format.
- Contrast: darker water, brighter coins and spirals in the vortex.
- Sound: a real music bed (bass line, pad chords, arpeggio, 120 BPM, locked to the existing beat) from the first content frame. It cuts out for tension before the plug and comes back after it. The hook is the loudest, densest stretch.

- Outro: the Attention Legend plays with its fanfare, then the last ~4 s go still and quiet (music fades, rays stop). That stretch takes the notebook's mandatory 20%, after the CTA, where it costs nothing with real viewers. This is a deliberate move for the chart, and the doc says so plainly.

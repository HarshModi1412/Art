## Problem Statement

`GO_TO_MARKET.md` v3 leads with a free Win-Back Session. The founder asked whether the idea holds for sellers who sell on Instagram and WhatsApp: "if someone bought a T-shirt from you, then sending them a message after 1 month and then after that every two weeks, is it a good idea or not?" If not, what should we do instead?

**Short answer:** no for clothing and jewellery, yes only for products that run out (attar, skincare). Message each buyer when there is a reason tied to what they bought, not on a timer.

## Demand Evidence

- None yet. No seller has been asked how they handle past buyers (D1: "haven't asked yet"). The "they have data but never act on it" idea in `us-pivot-five-seller-test.md` is also unheard from any seller.
- Outside benchmarks (retention-vendor blogs, directional only, not Indian or social-seller data):

([eightx](https://eightx.co/blog/average-time-between-orders-by-vertical), [RetentionLab](https://www.retentionlab.ai/blog/repeat-purchase-rate-measure-benchmark-improve))
  - Buyers quiet for 60 to 120 days are the most recoverable, and win-back there recovers about 4 to 8% of them. Post-purchase follow-up matters more than win-back for apparel. ([eightx](https://eightx.co/blog/average-repeat-purchase-rate-by-vertical))

For **WhatsApp Business Platform (API) numbers**, a block rate above about 2% lowers the quality rating and limits sending. Vendor guidance, not WhatsApp's own rule text. ## What the app does (re-checked in the working tree, 2 October 2026, 23:30 IST)

The campaign code was being changed by a parallel session while this design was written (`.planning/quick/261002-wg2-campaign-types`, uncommitted). Facts below are from that working tree.

- **Seven campaign types, each with its own audience** (`analytics.campaign_audience`, `campaign_engine.REASONS`):

  | Type | Audience |
  |---|---|
  | winback | At Risk plus Hibernating buyers quiet up to 365 days |
  | festival | Champions, Loyal, top 20% spend, At Risk |
  | second_order | one order only, 14 to 60 days ago |
  | cross_sell | owns X, not Y, for a strong "bought together" pair |
  | restock | past their usual reorder gap for a product |
  | vip | Champions |
  | thank_you | last order 2 to 14 days ago |

- **Each type has its own cooldown** (`campaign_engine.COOLDOWN`): winback 45, festival 14, second_order 30, cross_sell 21, restock 14, vip 14, thank_you 30 days. The wait is counted from the last contact by any campaign, but its length depends on the new campaign's type. So a festival offer can follow a win-back offer 15 days later. There is no single per-buyer offer limit across types.
- **"No discount" is now possible for every type**, using a silent tracking code instead of a visible one.
- **The default win-back voice is the "bad" message.** "It's {brand}... we've missed you", plus a 10% code (`campaign_writer` win-back voices; `winback_auto.DEFAULT_OFFER` 10%). A reason-based message exists only if the seller edits each row (`message_override`).
- **Sending paths:**
  - **Tap-to-send** (no WhatsApp API connected): each customer gets a `wa.me` link with the row's message, edits included. The seller taps send.
  - **Automatic** (WhatsApp Cloud API connected): sends a fixed approved template built from the voice's pitch. **Edits are ignored.**
  - **Email:** approving defaults to WhatsApp and email together. Email goes from the seller's mailbox and has **no unsubscribe link**.
- **The automatic batch is on by default.** `winback_auto` turns itself on at first check. Every second Monday it puts a win-back batch (up to 60 buyers, 10% offer) in the Approval panel, or a festival batch when a festival is within 21 days. It never sends without approval.
- **Restock** guesses the reorder gap from repeat purchases of the same product. Products nobody has bought twice get no reminder. There is no "this bottle lasts N weeks" setting.
- **The app cannot read Instagram DMs or comments.** Scopes are basic, publish and insights only (`backend/core/instagram.py:60`).
- **The app does not record opt-in.** Nothing stores whether a buyer agreed to repeat messages.

## Premises (agreed; founder chose "take all the recommended")

1. "Day 30, then every 2 weeks" is wrong for clothing and jewellery. A timer fits only products that run out (attar, skincare).
2. Every message needs a buyer-specific reason: a new drop in what they bought, a restock, the festival they bought for last year, a matching piece, or a thank-you and review request. No reason, no message.
3. Three money windows, ranked:
   - the first 30 days after a purchase (the second sale);
   - people who asked "price?" and never bought;
   - buyers quiet for 60 to 120 days (about 4 to 8% recovery).
4. A thank-you about their order is expected. Repeat offers and drop alerts need the buyer's YES. Silence counts as no.
5. The free session stays, but promises "find your next orders in your past buyers", not "message everyone every 2 weeks". It is a bet until 3 sellers describe how they handle past buyers.

## Recommended Approach: A, using B's campaign types as they ship

### Definitions

- **Offer message:** contains a discount, a code, or a direct "buy now" ask.
- **Update message:** a thank-you, a review request, a drop or restock alert with no discount, a refill reminder with no discount.
- **Reason:** something new and specific to that buyer: a new or restocked piece in their favourite category, a matching piece, last year's festival, a refill due, or their recent order.

### Message rules

| Message | When | Needs a prior YES? | Type in the app |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thank-you + review/photo request + YES ask ("Want a message when new [category] drops? Reply YES") | Order date + the seller's usual delivery days + 2 days (about days 4 to 10) | No: every buyer gets this once | thank_you |
| Second piece: a matching item or a new drop in their category | Days 14 to 60 after the order, only with a reason | No, once; it carries the YES ask. After that, YES only | second_order, cross_sell |
| Refill reminder (attar, skincare) | Past their usual gap (app restock list), or the seller's stated bottle life | No, once, with the YES ask. After that, YES only | restock |
| Festival message | 2 to 3 weeks before the festival, to buyers who ordered in the 30 days before last year's festival (Diwali 2025: 20 October 2025) | No, once, with the YES ask | festival, list filtered by order date |
| Win-back | Quiet 60 to 120 days (first sessions). Up to 365 days only with a strong reason | No, once, with the YES ask | winback |
| New drop broadcast | When something new arrives | **Yes** | vip or a hand-picked list |

### Limits on how often (per buyer)

- **Offer messages:** at most 1 per 30 days across all types. The app does not enforce this across types, so the seller checks the send log.
- **Update messages:** at most 1 per week.
- **Without a YES:** at most 1 message per type ever, plus the thank-you. No reply counts as no.
- **Stop rule:** 2 unanswered messages in a row, then stop. **A new order or any reply resets the count.**
- **Never:** the same text twice to one person, or a discount with no product reason.
- **Broadcasts:** send to YES buyers one by one (tap-to-send), not through a broadcast list. Broadcast lists only reach people who saved the seller's number.

### The send log and the YES record (no new features needed)

- **The send log:** in-app sends are logged by the app (`campaign_engine` history, `winback_proof`). Hand-sent messages go into a **session sheet** the founder keeps per seller:

  `buyer | date | type | reason | offer or update | try number | replied (Y/N) | ordered within 7 days (Y/N)`

- **The YES record:** a WhatsApp Business label "YES-drops", or a "YES" column in the seller's order sheet or the session sheet. Check it before any broadcast or repeat offer. An app-side opt-in record is a later feature.

### Session steps (Branch A, 15-minute timebox; replaces v3's Branch A steps)

1. **Current reasons (2 minutes).** The seller names what is new or restocked, by category, plus any festival within 3 weeks. If there is none, they photograph one piece now; that becomes the reason.
2. **Thank-yous first (3 minutes).** Open the thank_you list (or filter the upload to orders 2 to 14 days old). Send thank-you + YES ask to up to 5 buyers. This window ranks first, and the message is welcome, so it is the safest first send.
3. **Reasoned past buyers (8 minutes).** Open second_order, cross_sell, restock, or winback filtered to 60 to 120 days quiet. Keep only buyers whose favourite category matches a current reason. Take the top 10 by past spend. Set **No discount**, edit each row to name the reason, send by tap-to-send.
4. **Zero-reason fallback.** If steps 2 and 3 find nobody, send nothing to past buyers. Close on the thank-you habit: "Every buyer from now on gets this message on day 4."
5. **Log it.** Fill the session sheet during the call.

**Example messages:**

- Bad: "Hi! We miss you. Get 10% off your next order."
- Good: "Hi Priya, the indigo block-print kurta you took in March is back in two new colours. Want pictures?"

### Settings for every session

- WhatsApp: **tap-to-send only.** The automatic template ignores edits.
- Email: **unticked** in sessions. US email stays off until win-back emails carry an unsubscribe link.
- Automatic every-second-Monday batch: **switch it off during the session.** Reconsider at day 14 once the seller has run two types by hand.
- Price-askers (Approach C): sessions only ask the question. No coaching, so no rules are needed yet.

## Decision rules (after the first 10 sessions)

| What we see | Decision |
|---|---|
| Any seller reports a WhatsApp warning, restriction, or 2+ blocks | Stop offers to non-YES buyers entirely; updates only |
| In most sessions the seller can name a reason for fewer than 3 past buyers | Lead the session with thank-you and second-order, not win-back |
| Reason-based messages get replies in at least half the sessions | Keep the rules; set the first reply-rate target from the observed median |
| 3 of the first 5 sellers say price-askers are the bigger leak | Run an office-hours session on Approach C (manual follow-up first) |
| Status-quo answers show sellers already message buyers by hand with reasons | Change the pitch to "the list and the message, done for you every week" |

> The frequency, once-per-type, stop and YES rules apply to every send, but most sends happen after the call: the thank-you 'for every buyer from now on', the second campaign type run by hand by day 14, and broadcasts to YES buyers. The founder keeps the session sheet, yet the limits say 'the seller checks the send log'. Nothing says who logs the seller's own sends between check-ins, or where the seller sees that log.

**Remedy**

> Name the post-session log and its keeper. For example: the seller keeps the sheet and the founder reviews it at the day-4 and day-14 check-ins. List the checks the seller makes before each send.

### R2-18 — completeness

**Problem**

> 'Settings for every session' covers only the call. After it the seller runs campaigns alone, and the app's defaults point the other way. Send defaults to WhatsApp plus email. Win-back, second-order, cross-sell and festival default to an offer, flat or percent; the automatic batch uses 10%. A connected Cloud API sends the fixed template. The default win-back voice is the 'bad' message. The after-yes change tells sellers to run a second type by hand without giving them these settings.

**Remedy**

> Add the session settings to the after-yes handover as a short checklist the seller keeps: email unticked, No discount, every row edited with its reason and the YES line, tap-to-send only.

### R2-19 — completeness

**Problem**

> US Shopify sellers are the second target, but sessions are WhatsApp-only and US email stays off until an unsubscribe link exists, which the feature gate delays. Most US buyers are not on WhatsApp. v3's US workaround, 'export the list to Shopify Email / Klaviyo', goes unmentioned and would bypass the reason, YES and frequency rules. The design never says what a US session sends.

**Remedy**

> State the US path. Either US sessions pause until the unsubscribe link exists, or they export only reason-filtered lists to the seller's own email tool (which provides the unsubscribe link) under the same frequency rules.

### R2-20 — completeness

**Problem**

> The decision rules say what keeps the reason-based rules ('replies in at least half the sessions') but not what changes or drops them. No decision is set for replies in fewer than half the sessions, or for replies that bring no orders within 7 days. The 'drops' part of the round-1 obligation is still unmet.

**Remedy**

> Add the failure branch. For example: replies in fewer than half the sessions trigger a review of the message wording and list choice with sellers before the next 5 sessions (as v3's 'sent messages but got no replies' row did). Say which result would drop the session promise.

### R2-21 — completeness

**Problem**

> The first decision rule fires on '2+ blocks', and the day-4 check-in asks 'anyone block you?'. WhatsApp does not tell senders when someone blocks them, so sellers cannot report blocks reliably and this trigger may never fire.

**Remedy**

> Use signals sellers can observe: a WhatsApp warning or restriction notice, or buyers replying 'stop' or complaining. Or define a proxy, for example messages still at one grey tick after 7 days.

### R2-22 — consistency

**Problem**

> Step 1's fallback says that when nothing is new, the seller photographs 'one piece now; that becomes the reason'. The Reason definition requires 'something new and specific to that buyer' (new or restocked, matching, festival, refill, recent order). An existing piece photographed on the call is none of these. Step 3 would then message every buyer in that category: a send with a picture but no reason, against premise 2, and against Brand.md's honesty rule if the piece is presented as new.

**Remedy**

> Allow the fallback only for a piece that is genuinely new or restocked, or that matches each buyer's purchase. Otherwise go straight to step 4 and send nothing to past buyers.

### R2-23 — consistency

**Problem**

> The thank-you rule is 'Order date + the seller's usual delivery days + 2 days (about days 4 to 10)'. Session step 2 sends to the thank_you list, or to 'orders 2 to 14 days old'. That includes buyers whose parcel has not arrived (days 2 to 3) and buyers past the window (days 11 to 14).

**Remedy**

> In step 2, drop rows ordered fewer than delivery days plus 2 days ago. Or widen the rule to the app's 2-to-14-day window and say why.

### R2-24 — clarity

**Problem**

> The thank-you row says 'every buyer gets this once'. The limits say 'at most 1 message per type ever, plus the thank-you', and 'A new order... resets the count'. It is unclear whether a repeat buyer gets a thank-you, and another YES ask, after every order or only after the first. The app's thank_you list includes repeat buyers and allows a thank-you every 30 days.

**Remedy**

> State whether the thank-you is once per buyer or once per order, and whether it repeats the YES ask to buyers who were already asked.

### R2-25 — clarity

**Problem**

> An offer message is defined as one that 'contains a discount, a code, or a direct "buy now" ask'. With No discount, the app still issues a code per buyer. When the shop is published, it appends a link carrying that code as ref= to every message, edited ones included (message_for adds the link when it is missing). Under the definition, every in-app 'update' from a seller with a published shop contains a code, so it counts against the 1-per-30-days offer limit.

**Remedy**

> Exclude silent tracking links from 'code' in the definition (for example: 'a visible discount code'). Say whether a shop link alone counts as a 'buy now' ask.

### R2-26 — clarity

**Problem**

> The win-back row allows buyers 120 to 365 days quiet 'only with a strong reason'. Every message already needs a reason, and 'strong' is not defined. The 'Good' example writes to a buyer who bought in March, about 200 days before these October sessions, which puts it in that undefined zone.

**Remedy**

> Define a strong reason (for example: the exact item or its restock, or last year's festival), or drop the 120-to-365-day exception for the first sessions. Change the example to a buyer 60 to 120 days quiet, or label it as a strong-reason case.

### R2-27 — clarity

**Problem**

> 'Automatic every-second-Monday batch: switch it off during the session. Reconsider at day 14' does not say whether the batch stays off after the call, who reconsiders, or what must be true to turn it back on. Turned back on unchanged, it returns with the 10% DEFAULT_OFFER, the 'we've missed you' voice, and festival batches chosen without reasons.

**Remedy**

> State that the batch stays off after the session. Give the day-14 condition and the settings for turning it back on (offer none; every row edited or removed before approval), or keep it off until the gate is met.

### R2-28 — clarity

**Problem**

> The feature gate counts sellers who 'sent at least 5 reason-based messages and reported replies at their day-4 check-in'. It is unclear whether a seller who reports zero replies counts, which decides whether new features can unlock despite a poor reply rate.

**Remedy**

> Reword to 'reported their reply count (zero included)', or require at least one reply, and say which.

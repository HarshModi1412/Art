## Problem Statement
On 2026-09-26 the founder moved One Tap Manager's target customer from small Indian
D2C sellers to small US online sellers (clothing, jewelry, fragrance on Shopify,
Amazon and Instagram), because US sellers have more purchasing power. New pricing,
already built: a 7-day Pro Max trial with no card and nothing free after it, then
Pro at $10 a month (everything except AI image generation) or Pro Max at $12.99
(adds AI product photos and clips); India keeps ₹700 and ₹1,299. The question for
this session: is the pivot grounded, and what should happen next?

## Demand Evidence
- None yet, in either market. The founder chose "No US seller yet; the reason is
  purchasing power and price." The pivot is a market-level bet, not a response to a
  seller.
- What will count is defined under Success Criteria: a qualifying action and an
  unprompted return, both measured from the product's own records.

## Status Quo
- A one-person US Shopify store covers each job with a free or cheap single-purpose
  tool. Checked 2026-09-26 in web search results, not on each vendor's pricing page:
  Shopify Email (free tier) for win-back; Stockbot and Merchbees low-stock apps (free
  plans listed on the Shopify App Store); Photoroom from $12.99 a month; Pixelcut Pro
  from $9.99; Meta Business Suite for Instagram scheduling (free). Recheck each on its
  own pricing page before quoting it on the site.
- Hypothesis, not yet heard from a seller: they have the data but never act on it,
  because nobody has the time. The five calls test this by asking what they use today
  and what they last did about customers who stopped buying.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge
- No named person yet (founder: "Not yet"). Working hypothesis: a one-person US
  Shopify brand, roughly $3k to $15k a month, who packs orders themselves.
- Wedge (agreed, P2): "the morning list that does the work": win-back and reorder
  cards built from Shopify orders, with the email and the purchase order already
  written. Not the all-in-one bundle, and not AI photos.

## Constraints
- The product exists and ships: 14 modules, Shopify and Amazon US connectors, Today
  screen, sample data. Deploys to Render are manual.
- The Shopify connector (`backend/core/commerce.py`) backfills 90 days of orders by
  default (capped at 10,000), through the REST Admin API pinned to version `2024-07`,
  with a merchant-created custom-app token.
- Decisions the founder kept today, despite the counter-case (recorded, not re-argued):
  - The 7-day trial starts at signup (P4 declined).
  - The first run stays the current journey (P3 declined).
  - Razorpay charges US subscriptions in USD (P5 declined).

## Premises
1. (Agreed) The US pivot is a bet, not a finding. Before building more US-only
   features, get five US sellers onto the product and watch them.
2. (Agreed) The US wedge is the morning list that does the work. Lead with it.
3. (Declined) Rebuild the first run for US sellers: connect Shopify, then Today, in
   under 3 minutes.
4. (Declined) Start the trial clock when data arrives, or make it 14 days.
5. (Declined) Bill US sellers through Stripe or Shopify app billing.

## Approaches Considered
### Approach A: Five-seller test (chosen)
No new product features. Recruit five US one-person Shopify stores. Each seller signs
up and connects their own store while the founder watches silently on a call, then
the founder observes days 1 to 7 from the product's records. Effort S. Risk low.
### Approach B: Lead with the morning list
Rewrite the US home hero, pricing and the win-back and stock feature pages around the
wedge, with a live sample-data demo. Effort M. Deferred until A reports back.
### Approach C: Shopify App Store listing
Rejected for now: weeks of build and app review before any demand, and it conflicts
with the kept Razorpay decision.

## Preconditions (before the first outreach message)
1. **Shopify token path works in 2026.** Create a test development store, then issue an
   Admin API token for it the way a seller would. Confirm `shopify_pull` returns
   orders on the currently supported API version (the code pins `2024-07`, which is
   likely out of Shopify's support window). Write the exact click-by-click steps and
   the minimum scopes (`read_orders`, `read_products`, `read_inventory`, `read_customers`)
   into a one-page guide. If merchant-created custom apps can no longer be made, stop:
   the test needs a different connection path first.
2. **Win-back email is legal to send in the US.** CAN-SPAM needs an unsubscribe link,
   the sender's physical postal address and honored opt-outs. No unsubscribe handling
   was found in the send path (`campaigns.py`, `winback_proof.py`). Until that exists,
   sellers export the list and send from their own email tool, and the qualifying
   action below counts "exported and sent" as well as "sent from the app".
3. **Approving a purchase order has a real side effect.** It emails the seller's real
   supplier from the seller's own address. Tell each seller this before they approve one.
4. **Deploy and verify.** Deploy today's changes to Render (pricing and trial, region
   detection, US home at `/`, India home at `/in`, the trial gate, Pro Max image
   gate). Then, from a US browser or a VPN: `/` shows $10 and $12.99, a new signup
   has `billing.currency` = USD and a 7-day trial, and `LAUNCH_MODE` is not `true`
   in the Render environment. Confirm a test account whose trial is backdated past
   7 days gets the plan picker.
5. **Payments ready before signups.** Razorpay International Payments approved, and
   one successful USD test checkout. If approval is not in when the first trial would
   end, the founder extends that seller's trial by hand, and "paid by trial end" is
   scored from the extended date.
6. **Consent and data.** The seller pastes their own token (the founder never handles
   it). Tokens are stored encrypted (`secrets_store`, Fernet). Before the first call,
   check that the privacy policy covers US sellers' customer data, and tell each
   seller they can delete the account (and the token) from the Account tab at any time.

## Measurement (from records, not memory)
- Account and trial: `billing_account` in the user store (trial start, currency).
- Win-back sent: `winback_proof.mark_sent` records; if sent outside the app (see
  precondition 2), the seller's own screenshot or export log.
- Purchase order approved: the order's status in Supply.
- Return visits: session starts per day. The product records login sessions, not
  every visit. If that proves too coarse, add one log line per Today screen view
  before the first call. This is the only code change allowed in Approach A.
- Checkout attempts: the pending-order record kept per account by `billing.create_order`.

## Recommended Approach
Approach A this week, scored by the table below. Move to B only if the wedge row
passes and no confound fired for the sellers who failed.

| Outcome (per seller, observed) | What it means | Next step |
|---|---|---|
| Qualifying action by day 7 | Wedge works for them | Count toward the 3 |
| Reached Today, cards shown, no action by day 7 | Wedge miss | Count against the wedge |
| Stalled in onboarding before Today | First-run confound (P3) | Exclude from the wedge count, reopen P3 if 2 or more |
| Today had no actionable card on day 1 (thin data) | Data confound | Exclude from the wedge count; note the store's order volume |
| "Didn't get to it" before trial end | Trial-length confound (P4) | Exclude from the wedge count, reopen P4 if 2 or more |
| Tried to pay and could not | Payment confound (P5) | Reopen P5 on the first one |

The wedge is judged only on sellers with no confound. If fewer than 3 such sellers
remain, recruit more until 3 are clean, rather than concluding from a smaller number.

## Open Questions
- Which Today card does a US seller act on first: win-back, reorder or content?
- Does a $3k to $15k a month store have enough lapsed buyers and stock movement in 90
  days to fill the Today screen? The day-1 data-confound row answers it.

## Success Criteria
- Qualifying action: a win-back email actually sent (from the app or, per precondition
  2, exported and sent from their own tool) or a purchase order approved, done after
  the setup call ends and without being prompted.
- Unprompted return: a session on day 2 or 3, before the founder's check-in. The
  founder checks the records first, then messages the seller on day 4, never earlier.
- The decision window is day 7 (trial end) for both the action and the payment.
- Pass: of the sellers with no confound (at least 3, recruiting more if needed), at
  least 60% take a qualifying action by day 7, at least half return unprompted, and
  at least 1 pays (Pro or Pro Max) with no discount.
- Target date: 5 sellers connected by 2026-10-03. If fewer, extend by one week and
  keep the same percentage thresholds.

## Dependencies
- Preconditions 1 to 6 above, in order. Precondition 1 blocks everything.

## The Assignment
First, do precondition 1 today: make a development store and confirm a seller can
still create the token this connector needs. Then message US one-person Shopify stores
selling clothing, jewelry or fragrance, found through their own stores and Instagram
bios (not Reddit self-promotion: r/shopify and r/smallbusiness restrict it), and offer
a 20-minute setup call. Plan on about a 5% booking rate: send 100 messages and keep
going until five are booked. On each call, the seller signs up on their own device and
pastes their own token while you watch and say nothing. Write down their exact words.
Check the records on day 3, message them on day 4.

## Reviewer Concerns

### R2-1 — consistency

**Problem**

> Recommended Approach says 'Move to B only if the wedge row passes and no confound fired for the sellers who failed', but the table and the next paragraph exclude confounded sellers and judge the wedge only on clean sellers, recruiting more until 3 are clean. Under the first rule, any confound on a failing seller blocks B even when the clean sample passes. Under the second rule, that seller is simply excluded.

**Remedy**

> Pick one rule. Either B depends only on the clean-seller pass criteria, or a named confound (for example P5) blocks B until it is fixed. State it the same way in Recommended Approach and Success Criteria.

### R2-2 — consistency

**Problem**

> The table's pass row says 'Count toward the 3' (an absolute count), while Success Criteria pass at 'at least 60%' of clean sellers (at least 3) and 'at least half' return unprompted. With 3 or 4 clean sellers, 60% is 1.8 or 2.4 and half is 1.5 or 2, and no rounding rule is given. It is also unclear whether 3 clean sellers passing 2 of 3 is a pass, when the table implies 3 actions are needed.

**Remedy**

> State the pass thresholds as whole numbers for each clean-sample size (for example 3 clean: 2 act, 2 return; 5 clean: 3 act, 3 return), and make the table's 'Count toward the 3' match.

### R2-3 — clarity

**Problem**

> The 'Didn't get to it' trial-length confound can only come from what the seller says, so a seller who reached Today, saw cards and did nothing (a wedge miss) can be moved into an excluded confound by their own excuse. The table also gives no precedence when one seller fits more than one row (for example, thin data and 'didn't get to it'), or acted and also failed to pay.

**Remedy**

> Define the observable evidence that separates 'didn't get to it' from a wedge miss (for example, no session after day 1 against sessions with no action), and add a precedence order for sellers who match more than one row.

### R2-4 — clarity

**Problem**

> The payment-confound row ('Tried to pay and could not', reopen P5 on the first one) does not say whether that seller is excluded from the wedge count or from the 'at least 1 pays' criterion. If the only seller willing to pay hits a checkout failure, the test fails on payment plumbing, not on demand.

**Remedy**

> State how a payment-confounded seller is scored: whether their action still counts for the wedge, and whether a confirmed but failed attempt to pay counts toward the pay criterion or triggers a retry once P5 is fixed.

### R2-5 — clarity

**Problem**

> The qualifying action must be 'without being prompted', but the founder messages every seller on day 4, and the decision window runs to day 7. The document does not say whether an action taken on days 4 to 7, after that message, counts.

**Remedy**

> State whether post-day-4 actions count, count separately (for example 'prompted action'), or are excluded, and what the day-4 message may and may not say, so it does not nudge the seller toward a card.

### R2-6 — clarity

**Problem**

> Day numbering is not defined. The trial starts at signup, the setup call is on signup day, and the criteria use 'day 1' (data confound), 'day 2 or 3' (unprompted return), 'day 3/4' (check and message) and 'day 7' (trial end). It is unclear whether signup day is day 0 or day 1, so a return on the calendar day after signup may or may not count.

**Remedy**

> Define signup day (for example, day 0 = signup and call; day 7 = trial expiry timestamp) and express each window against it, in the seller's time zone or UTC.

### R2-7 — consistency

**Problem**

> Preconditions are headed 'before the first outreach message' and Dependencies say 'Preconditions 1 to 6 above, in order', but The Assignment says to do precondition 1 today and 'Then message' stores. That skips preconditions 2 to 6, including deploy/verify and payment readiness.

**Remedy**

> Make The Assignment follow the gate: after precondition 1, complete 2 to 6 (or name which ones can safely run in parallel with outreach) before the first message.

### R2-8 — consistency

**Problem**

> Precondition 5 ('Payments ready before signups', a pre-outreach gate) requires Razorpay International Payments approval and a USD test checkout. Its own fallback assumes approval may still be missing when a trial ends. Both cannot hold. If approval really is a gate, an approval that takes weeks also makes the 2026-10-03 target unreachable, which the timeline does not acknowledge.

**Remedy**

### R2-9 — feasibility

**Problem**

It also does not say whether the day-7 action and return window moves with the extended trial, or only the payment date does.

**Remedy**

### R2-10 — consistency

**Remedy**

### R2-11 — feasibility

**Problem**

> Win-back cards need customer emails and names, which Shopify treats as protected customer data. Newer app types need explicit protected-data access for those fields. Precondition 1 checks that orders come back, but not that customer email fields are populated for a token made the way a seller would make it. Without them, win-back cards are empty or unusable, which would look like thin data.

**Remedy**

> In precondition 1, confirm that the pulled orders and customers include email and name for the test store under the documented scopes. If protected-data approval is needed, add that step to the one-page guide.

### R2-12 — feasibility

**Problem**

> Precondition 2 routes US win-back through 'sellers export the list and send from their own email tool', but the document does not confirm that the product has a win-back list or draft export. It also does not say how the exported list and drafted email reach the seller's tool.

**Remedy**

> Confirm an existing export (format, fields, where it is in the UI) before the first call, or state the manual method (for example, copying the draft and CSV) and whether using it counts as the seller acting unprompted.

### R2-13 — feasibility

**Problem**

> Precondition 3 says approving a PO 'emails the seller's real supplier from the seller's own address'. If the app sends through its own mail server with the seller's address in From, the seller's domain SPF and DMARC checks will fail, and the PO may be rejected or land in spam with no signal to the seller. The document does not verify how that send actually works.

**Remedy**

> Verify the PO send mechanism (From as the app with Reply-To the seller, or sending through the seller's mailbox) with a real test send, and describe what the seller and supplier actually see.

### R2-14 — feasibility

**Problem**

> Reorder cards produce a purchase order to a supplier, but supplier contacts are not in Shopify order data. A freshly connected seller probably has no supplier on file, so the reorder half of the wedge cannot be acted on in week 1 unless the supplier is entered, and the document does not say who enters it or when.

**Remedy**

> State whether supplier details are entered during the setup call (by the seller, unprompted or with a guide) or later, and how a seller with no supplier on file is scored for the PO path.

### R2-15 — completeness

**Problem**

The fix is conditional ('If that proves too coarse'), with no test or decision point, so the criterion may be unmeasurable once the test is running.

**Remedy**

> Decide before the first call whether login sessions capture a day-2 or day-3 return for a persistently logged-in user (check session lifetime). If they do not, commit to the Today-view log line now.

### R2-16 — completeness

**Problem**

> The table scores 'Reached Today, cards shown' and 'Today had no actionable card on day 1', but Measurement lists no record of reaching Today or of which cards were shown to each seller. The data confound and the wedge-miss row therefore cannot be told apart from records.

**Remedy**

### R2-17 — completeness

**Remedy**

### R2-18 — consistency

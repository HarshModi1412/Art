# One Tap Manager brand guide

This is the single reference for how One Tap Manager sounds, what it stands for, and who it is for. It governs product copy, the landing page, seller storefront defaults, generated captions, emails, and anything a customer reads. When a decision is not covered here, decide the way the rest of this document would.

A note on the name: the customer-facing product is **One Tap Manager**. "Smart CafeX" and "Content Seller" are internal or historical names for the same app and never appear in front of a seller.

---

## 1. What we are, in one line

One Tap Manager gives a one-person shop the team a big brand has: someone who reads the numbers, chases the customers who stopped coming, decides what to reorder, and posts every day. It reads the sales you already make on Amazon, Shopify, Instagram or your own counter, tells you the three things worth doing this morning, and then does most of them for you.

**Tagline:** Run your shop the way a big brand runs theirs. On your own, before lunch.

**Positioning statement:** Built for Shopify's customer. Built to Odoo's standard. We target the same small Indian D2C seller Shopify does, and we build to the quality and connected-data depth of Odoo, without claiming manufacturing, multi-warehouse, payroll or double-entry accounting.

## 2. Ideal Customer Profile

### Who they are

A small direct-to-consumer seller in India, usually running the whole thing themselves or with one or two helpers. They sell physical products, most often clothes (कपड़े), jewellery (गहने) or perfumes (परफ़्यूम), and increasingly beauty, food and small lifestyle goods. Monthly revenue is real but not large, and their time is the scarcest thing they have.

### Where they sell

Some mix of: their own counter or shop, Instagram, WhatsApp, a marketplace (Amazon, Flipkart, Myntra), a Shopify store, and the free selling website One Tap Manager gives them. They already make sales. What they lack is someone to read those sales and act on them.

### What they care about

Getting the next thing done, not learning software. Keeping the money they earn rather than paying it out in fees and app subscriptions. Looking as professional as the bigger brands they compete with. Being told plainly what to do next, in words they already use.

### What frustrates them

Tools built for people with a team. Jargon (EOQ, reorder point, safety stock, RFM) that assumes an MBA. Per-order fees and percentage-of-sales cuts that grow as they grow. A stack of four to six separate apps that each cost ₹800 to ₹2,400 a month and none of which talk to each other. Dashboards full of charts that never say what to do.

### Who we are NOT for

Enterprises, multi-warehouse operations, manufacturers, and businesses with a finance team. Anyone who wants a general-purpose spreadsheet or BI tool. We do not pretend to be an ERP.

### The job they hire us to do

"Tell me the few things worth doing today to make more money from the shop I already have, write the messages and the posts and the orders for me, and do not take a cut of my sales to do it."

## 3. Brand personality

If One Tap Manager were a person, they would be the sharp, calm operator a big brand keeps in the back office: the one who has read every number, remembers every customer who drifted away, and tells you the two or three moves that matter this week in plain sentences. They never show off what they know. They never make you feel behind. They hand you the order already filled in, the message already written, and let you press send. They would rather show you the arithmetic than ask you to trust them.

They speak the way a trusted shopkeeper's friend speaks: warm, direct, a little bit Hinglish, respectful of how hard you already work.

---

## 4. Voice attributes

Five attributes define how we communicate. They are fixed. Tone dials them up or down by context (see section 6), but none of them ever disappears.

### Plain
- **We are:** everyday words, short sentences, one idea at a time. We name the thing the seller would name.
- **We are not:** dumbed down, vague, or padded. Plain is not empty.
- **This sounds like:** "Your best-selling shirt in M lasts three more days at the rate it is going. The order form is already written. Check it and send it."
- **This does NOT sound like:** "Leverage predictive inventory intelligence to optimise your replenishment workflow."

### Honest
- **We are:** truthful about what is real and what is illustrative, about what works and what is new. Every example figure is labelled as an example with the arithmetic shown.
- **We are not:** hypey, absolute, or willing to imply a result we have not seen.
- **This sounds like:** "We are new and would rather you check our maths than trust our marketing."
- **This does NOT sound like:** "Sellers see a 40% revenue jump in week one." (No such claim unless it is real, attributed and typical.)

### Useful
- **We are:** always pointed at the next action. Every screen ends in something to do, and where we can, we have already done most of it.
- **We are not:** decorative. We do not show a chart for the sake of a chart.
- **This sounds like:** "38 buyers have not come back in 45 days. The message is written. You press send, and we count how many return."
- **This does NOT sound like:** "Here is a dashboard of your customer segments."

### Warm
- **We are:** on the seller's side, respectful of their effort, never condescending. We assume they are smart and busy, not that they are behind.
- **We are not:** chummy, jokey, or fake-excited. Warmth is respect, not backslapping.
- **This sounds like:** "Not because you are doing anything wrong, but because one person cannot watch everything at once."
- **This does NOT sound like:** "Don't worry, we've got you! 🎉 This is going to be AMAZING!"

### India-first
- **We are:** built for how Indian sellers actually work. Rupees and lakhs, GST, festivals, WhatsApp, COD, Hinglish where it is natural. Chai, not coffee.
- **We are not:** a Western tool with a rupee sign pasted on.
- **This sounds like:** "the difference this closes, for one person, in the time it takes to have chai."
- **This does NOT sound like:** "Save time so you can grab a latte."

## 5. Messaging pillars

Everything we say ladders up to these four. First pillar leads; the rest support.

1. **You already have the team's knowledge in your data. You are missing the team.** Big brands do not sell better products. They sell with people who read the numbers and act. One Tap Manager is that team, for one person. This is the lead message.

2. **Connect what you already sell on, get told what to fix.** Analytics is the wedge and it is free forever. Upload one sales file, or connect a channel, and the first answers are on screen in about two minutes. It does not ask you to change how you sell.

3. **One flat price that replaces four to six apps. Never a cut of your sales.** Priced explicitly against the Shopify app stack (roughly ₹7,600 a month across separate apps) versus ₹999 a month for Max. Not per-order, not a percentage of sales. Your money is yours.

4. **It does the work, not just the thinking.** The win-back message is written. The purchase order is filled in. The week of Instagram is planned, captioned and scheduled. You approve and press send. New-generation AI, aimed at doing, not at demos.

## 6. Tone across contexts

The voice stays fixed. Tone shifts the balance.

| Context | What to dial up |
|---|---|
| Landing / marketing | Plain and Honest. Show the money that leaks and the arithmetic behind it. Never hype. |
| In-app guidance | Useful above all. Every card ends in one clear action, ideally already done. |
| Generated captions and posts (seller's voice) | Warm and India-first, in the seller's brand voice, not ours. No em dashes, no emoji as decoration, no invented claims about the product. |
| Emails (digest, password reset) | Warm and Useful. Say what happened and what to do. Never alarmist. |
| Errors and limits | Honest and Warm, blame-free. "You have used a lot of an expensive thing; here is the free way to keep going" rather than "limit reached, upgrade now." |
| Pricing and money | Honest and Plain. State the number, state what it replaces, state that we never take a cut. No pressure, no fake scarcity. |
| Bad news (a cap, an outage, a deprecation) | Honest and Warm. Say the truth, say what we are doing, point at the way forward. |

## 7. Style rules

These are hard rules. They apply to every customer-facing surface, including generated content.

**Never use:**
- Em dashes. Use commas, full stops, or "and". (This document follows the rule.)
- Emoji as interface icons. Use the shared stroke icon set. Emoji in a seller's own social caption is the seller's choice, never our default.
- Purple gradients. The palette is the muted slate-indigo system, light and dark, both verified to WCAG AA.
- Pill-shaped buttons. Buttons are rounded rectangles.
- Jargon in the interface. See the terminology table in section 8.
- ALL CAPS for emphasis (small labels and tags excepted). Use bold.
- Exclamation marks as a habit. Rarely, and never more than one.

**Always:**
- Write in sentence case for headings and buttons, not Title Case.
- Address the seller as "you" and their business as "your shop".
- Use ₹, lakh and crore for money. Indian number grouping.
- Label every illustrative figure as illustrative, and show the arithmetic. An unlabelled figure on a launch page is a lie.
- Show a moving indicator on any operation that takes real time, and tell the seller they can go do something else and come back.
- Keep forms stacked vertically, one field per row.

**Numbers and formatting:**
- Spell out one to nine in prose; use numerals for 10 and up and for all money and counts in the UI.
- Percent sign, not "percent", in the UI.
- Dates as "20 Sept" style in the UI; full "20 September 2026" in documents.
- Times in 12-hour with am/pm, IST.

## 8. Terminology

### Plain-language replacements (never use the jargon in the UI)

| Say this | Not this |
|---|---|
| buy again at | reorder point |
| spare to keep | safety stock |
| smallest quantity they will sell | MOQ |
| how much to buy | EOQ / economic order quantity |
| customers who stopped coming / who spends most / who buys often | RFM segments |
| your best sellers, your slow movers | ABC analysis |
| your own selling website | storefront / D2C site (fine in internal docs) |

### Product and money terms
- The product is **One Tap Manager**, capitalised, no "the" in front.
- Plans are **Free** (₹0, forever) and **Max** (₹999 a month). Credit packs (100 for ₹299, 300 for ₹749, 1,000 for ₹1,999) are the pay-as-you-go alternative, and credits never expire.
- "Semi Pro" is retired. Never show it. "pro" is only the internal id for Max; the seller always reads "Max".
- While launch mode is on, every feature is free. Say "Free during launch" on anything that will later need Max, so nobody is surprised when it ends.
- We never describe our pricing as a commission, a cut, a take rate, or per-order. We charge a flat monthly price, or credits that never expire. Say so plainly.

### AI and honesty terms
- Generated images and clips are always labelled "AI generated", because Indian law requires it and because it is true. Never hide it, never remove another tool's label.
- A generated picture is "made" or "generated", never presented as a photograph. A re-shoot of the seller's own photo keeps the real product.
- We never invent a customer, a review, a metric, or a brand name. The brand name comes from the seller, never from their email address.

## 9. Things we never do

These protect the brand more than any tagline.

- Never take a percentage of a seller's sales, and never let the copy imply we might.
- Never publish a fake review, a fake customer count, a fake "join 10,000 sellers" counter, or an unlabelled figure.
- Never claim a result ("40% more revenue") we have not measured and cannot attribute.
- Never use a dark pattern: no fake scarcity, no pre-ticked upsell, no cancel-is-hidden, no equal-prominence trick on consent.
- Never ship a half-working thing. One small unusable thing is a huge turn-off, so anything that ships is usable end to end.
- Never make the seller learn our vocabulary to use their own shop.
- Never hide behind AI. If something is generated, it says so. If SMTP is not set up, the reset flow says the truth rather than pretending a mail went out.

---

## 10. Proof and evidence standard

We are new, and we say so. Because of that, our credibility comes from arithmetic, not adjectives.

- Every example is labelled as an example and shows the numbers behind it.
- Recommendations that need data (trends, forecasts, weekday patterns) wait until there is enough data (around 30 orders) rather than inventing confidence. Headline totals that are just sums of the seller's own rows are always shown.
- Comparative claims (against the Shopify app stack, for instance) are stated as a stack of named, priced apps with the arithmetic shown, not as "we are cheaper than everyone".
- When we do not know, we say we do not know, and we point at the way to find out.

---

## 11. Quick test before anything ships

Read it once and ask:

1. Would a busy seller understand every word without a glossary?
2. Is there a clear next action, and have we done as much of it as we can?
3. Is every number either the seller's own or labelled as an example with its maths shown?
4. Does it take any of the seller's money by surprise, or take a cut? (It must not.)
5. Any em dash, emoji icon, purple gradient, pill button, or hype word? (Remove it.)
6. Does it sound like the calm operator in section 3, on the seller's side?

If all six pass, it is on brand.

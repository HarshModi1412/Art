# How to read this document

This is the director's cut of `OTM-12-Posts-Simple.md`. Every post is rewritten the way a professional director hands a script to a crew: what the viewer should feel, where the camera sits, what the hands do, what the audience hears, what text appears, for how many frames, and what to check before it goes live.

The document has four parts.

1. **The review.** What is broken or risky in the current plan, and what this version fixes. Read this first.
2. **The production bible.** Rules that apply to every post: look, camera, light, sound, type, motion, safety, file naming. Read once, then refer back.
3. **The 12 scripts.** Seven reels and five carousels or statics, in posting order within each week.
4. **The appendices.** Shoot-day plan, prop list, AI prompt pack, the tracking sheet and the pre-publish checklist.

**Conventions used in every script**

- Timecodes are in seconds from the first frame, for example `0.0 to 0.5 s`.
- **OST** means on-screen text (burned into the video). **SFX** means a sound effect. **VO** means voice-over. **MX** means music.
- Shot codes read `P2-S03`: Post 2, Shot 3. Use them as file names on the shoot day.
- "Hands" always means the brand's faceless seller: medium-brown hands, a thin gold bangle on the right wrist, a red-and-yellow kalava thread on the left, a mustard kurta sleeve with cream piping.
- Nothing in this document shows a human face. People are seen from behind, from above, or from the shoulders down.
- Colour names refer to the brand tokens: **Night** `#111827`, **Ink** `#101828`, **Paper** `#F7F5EF`, **Paper 2** `#EFECE3`, **Indigo** `#1E3A8A`, **Marigold** `#F5A623`, **Money Green** `#34D399`, **Sky** `#A9C1F5`, **Leak** `#C4320A`.


# Part 1: The review (office hours)

I read the plan the way a partner at office hours would: what will actually happen when a real seller scrolls past this, and what will break on the shoot day. The creative is strong. The hooks are specific, the proof section uses real app footage, and the honesty tags are better than most funded brands manage. The problems are in consistency, a few claims, and one strategic question you should answer before you shoot anything.

## 1.1 The strategic question first

**You have zero customers and a goal of 20 sellers in 7 days (Post 12). Twelve posts on a new account will not get you there on their own.** A brand-new Instagram account typically gets a few hundred views per reel in its first weeks, and most of those viewers are not Indian small sellers. The 0 to 20 challenge will be won in the DMs, not the feed.

What that means for this plan:

- Treat the 12 posts as **proof you can send**, not as the engine. When you DM a boutique owner, you link Post 3 or Post 5 so she sees you are real and useful.
- Before each post goes live, have a list of 20 real sellers you will personally send it to. Instagram rewards early engagement, and your first 50 views should come from the people you are trying to win.
- The single number that matters for the next 3 weeks is **sellers who published a website**, not views. Put it on the 0/20 counter and nowhere else.

This does not change a single script. It changes what you do in the hour after each post goes live.

## 1.3 Creative logic: where the hook and the product do not connect

A hook earns the first 3 seconds. The **slip line** (the one sentence that connects the stunt to the seller's problem) earns the next 15. Three reels have a slip line that fights its own hook.

- **Post 4 (Dadaji).** Dadaji is calm on the drop. The slip line then says the seller's sales dropped and she did not know. Calm plus drop reads as "drops are fine", which is the opposite of the message. **Fix:** Dadaji is calm *because he knew the drop was coming*. New slip line: "Dadaji ko pata tha drop aane wala hai. Tumhe pata hai tumhari sales kab giri?"
- **Post 8 (sarees on the stairs).** The hook shows too much stock falling. The product is about stock running *out*. **Fix:** the joke is about the step she cannot see. New slip line: "Aakhri step dikhta nahi. Stock ka aakhri piece bhi nahi." Now the hook and the reorder alert say the same thing: you cannot see the end coming, the app can.
- **Post 11 (mixie with no lid).** The link from chutney to reviews is a stretch. **Fix:** the lid is the size chart. New slip line: "Size chart hi lid hai. Nahi lagaya, toh returns ke chhinte." The proof section then shows the app finding "size" as the top complaint and recommending a size chart.

## 1.4 Brand consistency

- **The recurring cast is missing from the reels.** Your Brand Language makes "the seller from behind" (messy bun, yellow pencil, mustard kurta) the star of every reel hook. The Simple version uses a pakoda stall, Dadaji, a man on a stool and a stranger on the stairs. Recurring characters are how small accounts get recognised in the feed. **This version keeps the stunts but puts the seller's hands, bangle and kalava in every hook where it is safe to do so**, and puts her (from behind) in Posts 8 and 11. Dadaji and the man on the stool are kept as guest characters.
- **Stock footage with faces.** The Pexels drop-tower clips show real riders' faces. Legally fine under the Pexels licence, but it breaks the "no faces" look. Pick clips shot from behind or far away, or crop and blur.

## 1.5 The test-and-track table will not tell you what you think

The plan compares 7 reels to find the winning "hook style". Each reel differs in hook concept, pace, topic, CTA and posting day at the same time, and a new account's views swing a lot from random factors. With 7 data points you cannot say "slow motion beats machine-gun cuts".

What you *can* read: **3-second hold rate** (did the first frame stop the thumb) and **comments with the keyword** (did it produce leads). Use those two, treat them as directional, and do not kill a style on one reel. This version adds a "what changed" column to the tracking sheet so you can see confounds.

## 1.6 Feasibility for a one-person team

Seven practical stunts, props, a spotter, 240 fps slow motion, a balcony and a stall, in three weeks, while also doing 20 seller onboardings. That is a lot. Appendix A batches all filming into **3 shoot days** by location, so you set up each location once.

## 1.7 What is already strong (keep it)

- Price or danger on screen in frame one. This is the right instinct.
- Real screen recordings with SAMPLE SHOP DATA tags. This is your trust advantage.
- Post 3 and Post 5 give away something useful even to people who never sign up. These will be your most saved posts.
- Post 12's honesty ("we have zero customers") is the most followable post in the set. Keep it pinned.
- Comment-keyword CTAs. Make sure a comment-to-DM tool is live before Post 1, or block out 30 minutes after each post to reply by hand. An unanswered keyword comment is worse than no CTA.


# Part 2: The production bible

These rules apply to every post. When a script is silent on something, do what this section says.

## 2.1 The look in one sentence

**A real Indian home shop, filmed on a phone, in morning window light, where something expensive is about to go wrong, then a calm ledger-paper world where the app fixes it.**

Two visual worlds, never mixed in one frame:

- **The Hook World** (0 to about 5 s): real places, real textures, handheld phone camera, natural sound. It must look like a clip a friend sent you, not an ad.
- **The Ledger World** (5 s to the end): flat cards in Night, Paper and Indigo, the app's real screen recordings inside a plain black phone frame, the marigold highlighter, hard cuts on the beat.

The move from one world to the other is always the same transition: a **hard cut to a Night card** with a single bass hit. Viewers learn the pattern by the third reel.

## 2.2 Camera package (phone only)

| Item | Setting or choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Phone | The best rear camera you have access to. Use the main (1x) lens for everything except macros. | The 1x lens has the best sensor and the least distortion. |
| Resolution and frame rate | 4K at 30 fps for normal shots. 1080p at 240 fps for the one slow-motion shot (Post 8). | 4K gives room to crop and punch in during the edit. |
| Shutter | Double the frame rate where your app allows it (1/60 at 30 fps). | Natural motion blur. Fast shutters look like a video game. |
| Exposure and focus | Lock both (long-press on most phones) before every take. | Stops the image pumping when a hand enters frame. |
| White balance | Lock to daylight (about 5,500 K) indoors by the window. | Stops colour shifting between shots of the same scene. |
| Stabilisation | On for handheld. Off on a tripod. | Stabilisation on a tripod causes a slow drift. |
| Support | A cheap phone tripod with a clamp, plus a 1 to 2 m overhead arm or a broom handle taped across two chairs for top-down shots. | Top-down is used in 6 of the 7 reels. |
| Macro | Clip-on macro lens, or the phone's macro mode, for the oil, the necklace chain and the chutney. | Texture sells the danger. |
| Frame | Shoot vertical 9:16 for reels. Shoot 4:5 stills as vertical photos and crop. | No reframing losses. |

**Camera language (from the Brand Language, restated):** POV at 24 mm equivalent looking down at 60°; top-down at 90°; hero prop close-ups at desk height, 10° above; wide set at standing height, 30° down. Moves are slow: 5% push-ins, gentle handheld sway on POV. No drone moves, no spins. The only Dutch angle is on a comic "fail" beat.

## 2.3 Light

- **Day interiors:** the key is a window on camera left, 9 to 10 am. Switch off ceiling tubelights; they add green. Bounce with a white bedsheet on the right if shadows are too hard.
- **Night exteriors (Post 2 stall, Post 6 balcony):** use the location's own warm bulbs. Add one small LED panel at 3,200 K, bounced off a wall, if faces of objects go muddy. Never light it like a studio.
- **Grade:** highlights toward cream Paper, shadows toward Night navy, saturation at 90%, fine grain. Only the kalava red, the kurta mustard, marigold, and the hero object (the gold, the silk, the green chutney) are allowed to pop.

## 2.4 Sound

- **Record real sound on every hook take.** Phone mic is fine at 30 to 50 cm. The oil crackle, the chain on steel and the stool creak do half the work.
- **SFX kit** (keep them the same across all reels so the page has a sound): phone buzz, bass hit, cash-drawer "ting", soft "bonk", record scratch, low heartbeat, page flip, steel glass clink, keyboard click, "pop" for items appearing, "whoosh" for zooms.
- **Sonic logo** at the end of every reel: one soft finger tap on glass, then a warm two-note marimba chime rising a fifth, about 700 ms.
- **Music** only from Instagram's own audio library on the day of posting. Keep it at -18 to -22 dB under any voice, and drop it to silence at every key beat named in the scripts. Silence is the most powerful sound in these hooks.
- **Loudness:** final mix at about -14 LUFS.

## 2.5 On-screen text (reels)

| Element | Font | Size | Colour and box | Animation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hook line (0 to 5 s) | Inter ExtraBold 800 | 92 to 110 px | Paper text on a Night box at 85% opacity, 18 x 28 px padding, 14 px corner radius | Scale 92% to 100% with a small overshoot, 280 ms; out: fade 160 ms |
| Price or number | JetBrains Mono Bold | 140 to 200 px | Marigold on dark, Ink on light | Rolling digits over 600 to 900 ms |
| Proof lines | Inter Bold 700 | 64 to 72 px | Ink on Paper box, or Paper on Night | Same as hook line |
| Tags (SAMPLE SHOP DATA, AI GENERATED, ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE) | JetBrains Mono Medium, capitals | 24 to 26 px | Ink Soft on a Paper 2 chip | Static, on screen the whole time the relevant footage is |
| End card lines | Inter ExtraBold 800 | 64 to 80 px | Ink on Paper | Line by line, 120 ms apart |

**Rules:** maximum 7 words per line, 2 lines on screen at once. Sentence case. No em dashes, no emoji. Hinglish in Roman letters with the standard spellings (dukaan, kya, nahi, hai, khatam, wapas, hisaab).

**Safe zones on 1080 x 1920:** keep text out of the top 220 px, the bottom 420 px, and the right 150 px between y = 900 and y = 1700. Place hook text between y = 300 and y = 800 so the object of danger sits in the lower-middle of the frame.

**Reading time rule:** a line must stay on screen for at least 0.25 s per word plus 0.3 s. A 5-word line needs 1.55 s. Where a script beat is shorter than that, the line is split or shortened. All the scripts below already respect this.

## 2.6 Transitions (only three)

1. **Hard cut** on the beat. Default.
2. **Page flip:** a ledger page turns right to left in 450 ms. Used once per reel at most, from the "problem" to the "fix".
3. **Tap ripple wipe:** a marigold ring expands from the tap point to fill the screen with Paper in 500 ms. Used to enter the app recordings.

No spins, glitches, zoom-blurs, star wipes or 3D cubes. A "snap zoom" (a fast 100% to 130% scale on one element over 150 ms) is a move, not a transition, and is allowed on screen recordings.

## 2.7 The app recordings

| Code | What it shows | Used in |
|---|---|---|
| R1b | Sales analytics: last month's change, what sold less, next 30 days | Post 4 |
| R2 | Website builder: shop name, 3 products, publish (**to be recorded**) | Post 2 |
| R3 | Storefront: cart, checkout with COD and UPI (**to be recorded**) | Post 2 |
| R5 | Win-back: customers who went quiet, review and send | Post 5 |
| R6 | Plan a week of Instagram, festival campaigns | Post 6 |
| R7 | Reorder alert and purchase order | Post 8 |
| R8b | Pricing: Free and Max | Post 9 |
| R9 | Review and complaint analysis | Post 11 |

**Recording settings:** phone screen recorder at the highest quality, notifications off (Do Not Disturb), battery above 50%, clock set to 10:10, sample shop data loaded, a slow deliberate pace (you will speed it up 2x to 4x in the edit). Record each flow three times.

**Inside the edit:** place the recording in the plain black phone frame at 78% of frame width, centred, on a Night background. Every snap zoom lands on the exact number or button named in the script and holds for at least 0.8 s. The SAMPLE SHOP DATA chip sits top-left of the phone frame for the whole proof section.

## 2.8 The end card (last 2 s of every reel)

- Paper background.
- The 1T mark, 200 x 200 px, centred at y = 700, tap ripple animates once on the first frame.
- Line 1, Inter 800, 72 px, Ink, y = 960: the comment line (for example "Comment STORE.").
- Line 2, Inter 800, 52 px, Ink, y = 1060: "One dukaan problem. Fixed every week."
- Line 3, JetBrains Mono 500, 34 px, Ink Soft, y = 1140: "@onetapmanager"
- SFX: sonic logo. The last frame cuts cleanly back to frame 1 so the loop feels seamless.

## 2.9 Safety protocol (non-negotiable, every shoot)

1. **Stand-ins only.** Dummy or dead phones, imitation jewellery, cheap cloth, empty boxes. Nothing on set costs more than you can afford to lose.
2. **No heat near people or props.** Hot oil is filmed alone, from a distance, with nothing moving near it. Every "near the oil" shot uses a cold, empty kadhai.
3. **Every fall is under 30 cm, onto a mattress or mat, with a spotter out of frame.**
4. **No real edges.** Nobody stands near a balcony railing on a stool. The stool shot is filmed on the ground floor against a plain wall and composited, or the stool is under 30 cm tall and placed well back from the railing.
5. **Animals** (the cat in Post 6) are filmed separately, calm, and never near the action.
6. **Water and electricity:** the mixie in Post 11 is unplugged for every take except the one switch-press shot, and that shot uses plain coloured water.
7. **Consent.** Anyone who appears, even as hands, agrees to it. Dadaji says yes on camera off-record, not by assumption.

## 2.10 AI backup rules

Film for real wherever possible. Use AI only for a shot you cannot film safely or cheaply, and then:

- Generate the **first frame** as an image first (with your hand, kalava and khata reference photos attached), pick the most real-looking result, then animate it with image-to-video at exactly 10 s.
- Generate 3 or 4 takes and keep the one where the hands have five fingers and nothing melts.
- Every AI shot carries the **AI GENERATED** chip bottom-left and Instagram's AI label is switched on.
- If the AI changes an app screenshot or misspells a word, put the real screenshot or text on top in Canva or CapCut.

## 2.11 File naming and delivery

- Raw clips: `P{post}-S{shot}-T{take}.mp4`, for example `P2-S03-T2.mp4`.
- Edit project: `OTM-P{post}-v{version}`.
- Export: 1080 x 1920, H.264, 30 fps, high bitrate, AAC 48 kHz. Carousels: 1080 x 1350 PNG, sRGB.
- Cover frame for every reel: a separate 1080 x 1920 image with the hook line, designed so the centre 1080 x 1350 area reads on the profile grid.

### Director's intent

This is the post a stranger lands on when they tap the profile. It must work with no context. The first slide does all the emotional work: an empty chair behind six name plates is a picture of loneliness that a one-person seller recognises in half a second, and the "Salary? Ek cutting chai." line turns it into a joke before it gets sad. Every slide after that is one step of an argument, and the argument ends on money, because "we never take a cut" is the line that makes a seller trust the rest.

### Slide 1: the cover

**Purpose:** Stop the scroll on the profile grid and in the feed.

**Composition:** A straight-on shot at desk height, 10° above. The six name plates stand in one row across the middle third of the frame, the empty blue plastic chair centred behind them. The khata sits on the left edge, the chai glass on the right, both slightly cut off by the frame so the desk feels real and wider than the picture. The bottom 45% of the frame falls into a Night fade for the text.

**How to shoot it for real (recommended, 30 minutes):**

1. Make six name plates from folded cream card (A5 folded into a tent), with the job names printed in JetBrains Mono capitals at 28 pt, dark ink.
2. Put them on a wooden table against a cream or off-white wall. A blue plastic chair (the common Nilkamal kind) goes behind, pushed in slightly, empty.
3. Red cloth khata on the left, tied with string. Cutting chai glass on the right, two-thirds full, with a thin skin.
4. Light: a window on camera left at 9 to 10 am, no ceiling light. If the window has a grill, let its shadow fall on the wall behind the chair.
5. Phone on a tripod at desk height, 1x lens, focus on the name plates, exposure locked slightly under (so the fade to Night is easy).
6. Take 10 frames, moving the chai glass 2 cm between each. Pick the one where the chair sits exactly in the gap between plates 3 and 4.

**Name plates, left to right:** CEO · CA · PHOTOGRAPHER · PACKING · CUSTOMER CARE · INSTAGRAM

**Copy (exact):**

> Tu CEO bhi hai. CA bhi. Photographer bhi. Packing wala bhi.
>
> **Salary? Ek cutting chai.** (Marigold)

**Type spec:** Inter ExtraBold 800, 104 px, Paper, left aligned at x = 88, line height 1.02. The last line is on its own, in Marigold, with an extra 24 px of space above it. Break lines after "hai.", "bhi.", "bhi." so each job lands on its own beat.

**Tags:** `AI GENERATED` bottom-left only if you use the AI version. A real photo needs no tag.

### Director's intent

It must not look like a sticker sliding. Spend your time on two things: the cut-out edge of the car (clean, with the tyre smoke kept) and the moment the car crosses the post's border (add a soft shadow on the "caption" area as it passes, so it feels physical).

### Emotional curve

`Intrigue (0 to 1 s) → disbelief (1 to 2.5 s) → loss (2.5 to 4 s) → recognition (4 to 6.5 s) → relief (6.5 to 16 s) → action`

### How to build the illusion (step by step)

1. **Get the clip.** A top-down drone clip of a black car drifting on an empty lot:
   - [Drone View of a Black Car Drifting in a Parking Lot (Pexels)](https://www.pexels.com/video/drone-view-of-a-black-car-drifting-in-a-parking-lot-14912575/)
   - [Drone Footage of a Car in a Drift Park (Pexels)](https://www.pexels.com/video/drone-footage-of-a-car-in-a-drift-park-11180684/)
   - [Search: car drifting](https://www.pexels.com/search/videos/car%20drifting/)
2. **Design a fake feed in Canva (1080 x 1920).** Our own design, never Instagram's interface: a Paper background; a rounded post card (radius 24 px) at y = 360 to 1260 with a 1 px Paper 2 border; a small circle avatar and the handle `@yourshop` above it in Inter 600, 32 px; plain outline heart, comment and share icons below it (2 px stroke, our own icon set); two grey placeholder caption lines; the top edge of a second post card peeking in at y = 1500.
3. **In CapCut, layer 1:** the feed design.
4. **Layer 2:** the drift clip, masked to the post card's rounded rectangle.
5. **Layer 3:** a duplicate of the drift clip with **Remove background / Cutout** so only the car (and its smoke, as much as possible) remains. Unmasked.
6. **Keyframe layer 3:** from 1.0 s, as the car drifts wider in the clip, move the cutout car outward so it crosses the card's right border at 1.8 s, travels across the caption area and the second post by 3.2 s, and exits the right edge of the screen by 4.0 s. Add a soft drop shadow (Night, 25% opacity, 20 px blur, offset 12 px down) under the cutout so it sits "on" the feed.
7. **Freeze layer 2** on a frame of the empty lot from 2.5 s, so the post the car left is just empty tarmac with painted lines and a wisp of smoke.
8. **Smoke:** add a separate stock smoke overlay (screen blend mode, 60%) trailing behind the cutout as it crosses the feed.

# One Tap Manager: 12 Instagram posts (simple version)

**How to use this file**
1. Open your AI tool (ChatGPT image, Gemini, Ideogram for pictures; Veo, Kling, Runway for video).
2. Attach **OTM-Instagram-Brand-Language.md** every time.
3. Copy one prompt, paste it, and generate. One prompt = one slide or one clip.
4. If a prompt says "Attach", attach that file too. Screenshots are in the `screenshots` folder and recordings are in the `recordings` folder.
5. If the AI changes the app screenshot or misspells words, put the screenshot or text on top yourself in Canva.

Every picture made by AI needs a small "AI generated" tag in the bottom-left corner. The prompts already ask for it.

**How to get realistic video (read once)**
1. Make the **First frame** picture first, in your image AI, with your hand and khata reference photos attached. Pick the most real-looking result.
2. Give that picture to your video AI as the starting image (image-to-video in Veo, Kling, Runway or Hailuo), then paste the **Motion** prompt.
3. Every AI clip is exactly 10 seconds. Set the duration to 10 seconds in your tool (Kling, Runway, Hailuo and Veo all offer it, sometimes on a paid plan). If your tool only makes 8 seconds or less, generate the first half, then use its last frame as the start for the second half of the timeline.
4. Generate 3 or 4 takes and keep the one where the hands look right and nothing melts.
5. For a clip that continues the previous one, screenshot the last frame of the previous clip and use it as the first frame.
6. If the tool makes sound, keep it. If not, add the sounds listed in CapCut.

**How every reel works (read once)**
1. **0 to 5 sec, the hook.** Something expensive or alive is obviously about to go wrong, or something happens that looks impossible (a car drifting out of the feed). The price or the danger is on screen in the first second, so the viewer has to stay to see what happens.
2. **Each hook has its own pace** so the feed never repeats: 2x fast, slow motion (only one reel), machine-gun cuts, one unbroken take, a freeze-frame.
3. **5 to 6.5 sec, the slip line.** One line connects the danger to the seller's real problem.
4. **6.5 to 16 sec, the proof.** Fast cuts of the real app screen recordings, a zoom on each number, a sound on every cut.
5. **16 to 18 sec, the CTA.** "Comment WORD" on the paper-coloured card, with the 1T logo.
6. **Film the hooks for real** wherever you can (the "How to film" notes tell you how to keep it safe and cheap). Use the AI prompt only as a backup, and tag any AI clip "AI GENERATED".
7. **Safety, always:** every expensive thing is a stand-in (a dummy phone, imitation jewellery, cheap cloth), every fall is short, onto something soft, with a spotter out of frame, and no person or animal ever goes near heat or a real edge.
8. **Music:** pick from Instagram's own audio library on the day (it's licensed). Each reel names the track type and a fallback search word.

## Test and track: which hook style wins
Each reel uses a different hook style. After 72 hours, fill this in from Instagram Insights for each reel (tap the reel, then "View insights"):

| Post | Hook style | Pace | Views | Skip rate or 3-sec hold % | Avg watch time | Watched to the end % | Shares | Saves | Comments (keyword) | Follows |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| 4 | Rapid sequence: Dadaji drop tower | Cut every 0.4 sec | | | | | | | | |

| 11 | Danger: mixie with no lid | Fast, real speed | | | | | | | | |

**How to read it:**
- **Skip rate (or 3-second hold)** tells you if the hook works. Low skips means the first 3 seconds held people.
- **Average watch time** and **watched to the end** tell you if the middle and the slip line work.
- **Shares and saves** tell you if people found it worth sending or keeping. Shares matter most for reach.
- **Comments with the keyword** and **follows** tell you if it brought you leads.

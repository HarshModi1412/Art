Built for Shopify's customer. Built to Odoo's standard.

Two kinds of software already serve small sellers, and each gets half of it right.

Shopify

Right customer. A person who sells things, set up in an afternoon, no consultant.

Thin where it counts. The storefront is the product; the operations are apps. Inventory is an app. Win-back is an app. Reports are an app. Each is ₹800–2,400 a month, none of them talk to each other, and none of them know your GST position.

Odoo

Right depth. One data model. Purchase orders, inventory, invoicing and accounting are the same system, so a receipt moves stock and a sale posts tax without anyone re-keying it.

Wrong customer. Built for a business with someone to implement it. A jewellery seller with 40 SKUs will not get through the setup, and the UI assumes training.

One Tap Manager

Odoo's spine. Products, inventory, suppliers, orders, invoices and GST are one connected model — a website order drops stock, consumes materials, raises a compliant tax invoice and lands in your analytics without a second entry.

Shopify's front door. Sign up, upload a CSV or connect a channel, and the first screen tells you what to do this morning rather than asking you to configure a chart of accounts.

The difference in one sentence: Shopify tells you what happened and sells you an app to work out what to do. We tell you what to do, and the doing is already wired to the same data.
What we are not, yet. Odoo has manufacturing, multi-warehouse, payroll and double-entry accounting. We have none of those and are not pretending otherwise. If you have three warehouses and a factory floor, buy Odoo. If you sell 40 to 400 products and your accountant only wants a clean GSTR-1, that gap costs you nothing.

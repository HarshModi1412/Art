# The Pain to Goal Journey (Sellers With a Website)

**The chain, in order:**

> **PAIN** (what they feel today) → **FOMO** (what they are losing by staying there) → **GOAL** (the thing they started the business for) → **INSECURITY** (others already have it, and it is one login away) → **OUR SOLUTION** (what ships) → **THE TEST** (7 days, their own data)

This document adds the two missing links: the goal behind the pain, and the insecurity that makes FOMO sting. It builds on `Pain_Point.md` (pain, FOMO, offer map). The pains and facts are not repeated in full here.

---

## 1. What the end customer actually wants

Your read is that they want money and a big customer base. That is the headline, and it is right. The research adds a second layer that matters just as much, and that layer is where the emotional hook usually lives.

| # | What they want | In their own terms | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **G1** | **Money** | More sales, extra income, pay the bills, sometimes replace the day job | 39% of small business owners cite financial need as a start reason ([survey roundup](https://www.legalzoom.com/press-releases/small-business-outlook-2026)). 65% of Etsy sellers cite supplemental income ([Etsy seller data via Thunderbit](https://thunderbit.com/blog/etsy-stats)). **But** only 16% of side hustlers want it to become their main income ([Bankrate via Easy Apps](https://easyappsecom.com/guides/shopify-side-hustle-to-full-time)) |
| **G2** | **A customer base** | People who buy again, follow them, trust the brand | Seller posts: "customers don't come back" (S1 to S3, sourced pain list). A real base is what stops the sales rollercoaster |
| **G3** | **Being their own boss** | Autonomy, "my business, my rules" | The top reason in the 2025 to 2026 roundups: about 40% cite wanting to be their own boss ([survey roundup](https://www.legalzoom.com/press-releases/small-business-outlook-2026)) |
| **G4** | **Time and flexibility** | Evenings back, not working weekends, choose their hours | 35% cite flexibility. Etsy sellers rank flexibility among the top reasons ([Etsy Seller Handbook](https://www.etsy.com/uk/seller-handbook/article/creative-roots-understanding-etsy/22898443414)) |
| **G5** | **Pride and passion** | A brand that looks real, work they are proud of | 35% cite pursuing a passion. The top reason Etsy sellers give is a creative outlet |
| **G6** | **A business that stays manageable** | Grow, but not into a second full-time job | Etsy seller research notes many want more sales but want to keep the business a size they can run alone |
| **G7** | **To be taken seriously** | "A real business, not a hobby" | Inference from the posts about trust and a professional look (S6). Not a surveyed figure |

**Plain advice:** lead with G1 and G2 because they are the universal ones. Use G3, G4 and G5 as the emotional second line, because they are what the money is *for*. Do not promise G1 outright. A promise of money is an earnings claim in US advertising. We promise the access and the tools, and let them test.

*Evidence caveat:* I read the survey figures from search summaries and did not open every report. They are good enough to steer the story, not to print on screen.

## 2. The insecurity layer

FOMO says "you are losing something". Insecurity says "and others are not". The pattern for this audience:

> **Everything you want is one login away. The only difference between you and the seller who has it is that they logged in.**

**How to use it honestly:**
* It works on **access**, not on outcomes. "The tools are available to you today" is true. "Sellers who use this make more money" is a claim we cannot back, so we do not make it.
* Never name or invent competitors or fake sellers. Never shame the viewer.
* Frame the comparison around **behaviour**, using the sourced facts: "stores that follow up", "accounts that post 3 to 5 times a week". Those are real groups in real studies.
* The reassurance is as strong as the sting: "it takes one login" is the relief.

**Insecurity lines that stay inside the facts:**
* "Somewhere right now another seller is messaging the customers you've forgotten."
* "Accounts posting 3 to 5 times a week grow over twice as fast. How many times did you post?"
* "The seller who wins your customer back is one login away. So are you."

## 3. The full journey, pain by pain

Only the pains where we have a real answer. Pains 3, 8 and 10 stay out (we ship nothing for them), and pain 9 waits on a stock-sync check.

### Journey 1 (lead): "They buy once and never come back"

| Step | Content |
|---|---|
| **PAIN** | Customers buy once and never return. Sales stagnate. The seller keeps paying or working to find strangers (sourced: S1, S2, S3) |
| **FOMO** | Repeat customers are about 21% of a store's buyers but about 44% of its revenue ([Gorgias, 12,000+ merchants](https://www.gorgias.com/blog/repeat-customer-rate)). A customer who has gone quiet is 20 to 40% likely to buy again, a stranger only 5 to 20% (Paul Farris, *Marketing Metrics*, quoted by [Propello](https://propellocloud.com/blog/win-back-campaigns/)). The money is already in their customer list |
| **GOAL link** | **G1 money, G2 customer base.** This is the shortest path from "I have a store" to "I have customers who come back" |
| **INSECURITY** | "Another seller is messaging the customers you've forgotten. One login and it's you." |
| **OUR SOLUTION** | One Tap Manager reads their orders (Shopify or Amazon connection, file upload, or email intake), shows which customers are slipping away and what they spent, writes each message with the customer's favourite item and what goes with it, and counts who came back. Weekly batch prepared for them. **The seller taps send for each one. No automatic sending** |
| **THE TEST (7 days)** | Day 1 connect or upload orders. See your own number: how many went quiet, what they spent. Send your first 5 to 10 messages. Day 7: see who came back. They test the claim on their own data |
| **ANGLE** | "Find your quiet money" |

### Journey 2 (second): "I can't keep posting"

| Step | Content |
|---|---|
| **PAIN** | Keeping content going without a designer. Feed looks inconsistent, posting comes in bursts (sourced: S7, a fragrance brand) |
| **FOMO** | Accounts posting 3 to 5 times a week get about 12% more reach per post and more than double the follower growth of accounts posting 1 to 2 times ([Buffer, 2.1M posts, 2025](https://buffer.com/resources/how-often-to-post-on-instagram/)). This is a link, not proof of cause |
| **GOAL link** | **G2 customer base, G5 pride.** A feed that looks like a real brand and keeps showing up |
| **INSECURITY** | "Accounts that post 3 to 5 times a week grow over twice as fast. How many did you post this week?" |
| **OUR SOLUTION** | A week of Instagram planned, captioned and scheduled, then published; Product Studio learns their look from their own photos (AI photos and clips on Pro Max) |
| **THE TEST (7 days)** | Plan a week in minutes on day 1. Watch it post. See the week's reach for themselves. *(Instagram insights figures are demo until Meta review, so we do not promise live analytics.)* |
| **ANGLE** | "A week of posts, done before Monday" |

### Journey 3 (support): "Everything costs more than it should"

| Step | Content |
|---|---|
| **PAIN** | Apps and email tools cost too much for what they do (S8, S9, S10) |
| **FOMO** | Fees pile on top of the plan, sometimes charged per click or per sale. Money spent every month for tools never fully used |
| **GOAL link** | **G1 money (keep more of it), G6 manageable.** Lower costs and fewer tools |
| **INSECURITY** | "Some sellers keep what they earn. They just don't pay five apps to do it." *(Careful: no claim about what other sellers pay.)* |
| **OUR SOLUTION** | Pro $10 a month, Pro Max $12.99, no cut of sales, custom domain included. Win-back messages written for them so no flow builder is needed |
| **THE TEST (7 days)** | Add up their current app bill and compare it with $10 a month. A calculation they do themselves |
| **ANGLE** | "Add up your app bill" |
| **GUARDRAIL** | We only replace apps we truly replace. Be specific about which |

### Journey 4 (partial): "I don't know what actually earns"

| Step | Content |
|---|---|
| **PAIN** | Dashboard shows revenue, not what you keep. Backing the wrong product (S11, S12) |
| **FOMO** | Decisions made on a guess, putting money into the wrong product |
| **GOAL link** | **G1 money, G3 control.** Know where the money comes from |
| **INSECURITY** | "Your best seller might not be your best earner. Do you know?" |
| **OUR SOLUTION** | Sales Analytics and Sub-Category Analysis show what sold, what it earned and which categories bring the money; a forecast for next month; an AI analyst to ask questions |
| **THE TEST (7 days)** | Look at their own category breakdown on day 1 |
| **GUARDRAIL** | We do **not** subtract product cost, shipping, fees or ad spend, so never say "profit" or "what you keep" |
| **ANGLE** | Use as a supporting line only |

## 4. How this becomes one ad (the script skeleton)

Using your two frameworks, the journey maps straight onto the script:

| Script part | Journey step | Example for Journey 1 |
|---|---|---|
| **Hook (0 to 3s)** | PAIN, shown not told | "Your customers bought once. Then they vanished." |
| **Lock-in Zone (3 to 10s)** | FOMO (a fact) | "Repeat customers: 21% of buyers, 44% of revenue. A quiet customer is 20 to 40% likely to buy again. A stranger, 5 to 20%." |
| **Body** | GOAL link, INSECURITY, then OUR SOLUTION | "That's your next sale, already in your orders. Another seller is messaging them right now. One Tap Manager finds them, writes the message, counts who comes back." |
| **CTA** | THE TEST | "Try it free for 7 days. No card. See your own number." |

*Lock-in method:* Teasing the Proof (Method 1) for the facts, since we now have two traced numbers. The test replaces our own proof, so it is honest.

---

## 5. Guardrails for every line

* **No earnings promises.** We say "studies show" and "on average", and we let them test on their own data.
* **No claim about other sellers' results or spending.** Insecurity uses behaviour groups from real studies.
* **Features:** only what ships. No auto-WhatsApp sending, no real-time Instagram analytics, no profit figure, no review collection, no returns tool, no support inbox.
* **US only:** dollars and the US landing assets. Nothing in rupees.
* **Facts:** the Gorgias, Farris and Buffer numbers were traced. The goal surveys were read from summaries only.

## 6. Decisions for our discussion

1. **Goals:** do you agree with leading on money (G1) and customer base (G2), with freedom, time and pride (G3 to G5) as the emotional second line?
2. **Anything missing from the goal list?** For example, selling the business, building a brand worth something, or beating a competitor.
3. **Insecurity tone:** is "one login away" the right level, or do you want it sharper? I kept it to access and behaviour so it stays truthful.
4. **Order of ads:** Journey 1, then 2, then 3? Journey 4 only as a supporting line?
5. **The 7-day test:** is "see your own number on day 1, send your first messages, see who came back by day 7" the right promise?
